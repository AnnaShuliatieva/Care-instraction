Care Instructions for Tatting Lace Jewellery

Tatting lace jewellery requires delicate handling and care.
With proper care, your jewellery will retain its original appearance for decades.

Wearing

  • Put your jewellery on last, after applying make-up, perfume and hair products. Before putting on your jewellery, allow cosmetics and perfume to dry completely.
  • Avoid contact with perfume, creams and other harsh cosmetics.
  • Remove your jewellery before sleeping, physical activity, bathing, using a sauna or swimming pool, as well as before putting on or taking off clothing over your head.
  • Avoid contact between your jewellery and Velcro, zips, coarse woollen scarves and other surfaces that could snag the lace or cause friction.
  • When wearing your jewellery, be mindful of possible contact with seat belts, crossbody bags and backpack straps.

Storage

Tatting jewellery should be:

  • protected from contact with rough surfaces and sharp objects — this will help prevent mechanical damage (snags, tears, and scratches to the coating of beads or metal findings).
  • stored:
    • laid flat in its natural shape and in a horizontal position, without anything pressing down on it;
    • separately from other pieces, in a dark place, so that the jewellery is not exposed to direct sunlight during storage;
    • away from sudden changes in temperature.

These recommendations will help prevent changes in the colour of the lace and beads.

  • Jewellery should be stored in a way that prevents accidental exposure to water.
  • Do not leave jewellery on radiators or near other sources of heat.
  • Do not leave jewellery in a car exposed to direct sunlight.

The best storage option is a cardboard box with a soft lining.

If jewellery is stored for a long time in a tightly sealed bag or container, it should be taken out from time to time to allow the lace to “breathe”.

If the jewellery is not used for an extended period, a light wash once a year is recommended.

Keep jewellery out of reach of children and pets.

Shape and Structure

  • Do not pull or twist the lace, and do not try to “force it back into shape”.
  • Do not deliberately test the piece for strength: the lace is made of thread, and although it is quite strong, its strength has its limits.
  • Jewellery made using classic tatting will always be less rigid and therefore more prone to deformation.
  • If the jewellery becomes slightly deformed during storage, wear or transportation, its shape can be restored through a cycle of washing, ironing and steaming.

Washing

Mechanical friction is prohibited. Therefore, when washing the jewellery, any tools or methods involving rubbing or wringing must be excluded.

The use of alcohol wipes, acetone, jewellery cleaning products, stain removers, and any cleaning products not intended for lace is prohibited.

Jewellery must be washed by hand in comfortably hot water (approximately 38–42°C), hot to the touch but not scalding, using mild detergents without bleaching or other effects, without strong fragrances, and with a neutral pH. Avoid using hard water.

Jewellery should be washed as soon as barely visible traces of perspiration or small spots of any other dirt begin to appear.

  • Wet the jewellery under warm running water.
    • The metal findings must not be immersed in water.
  • Apply soapy lather to your fingers, hold the jewellery in your hands and apply the soap to the lace with your fingers.
    • Using gentle circular movements of your thumbs, carefully work over the surface of the lace on both sides, removing surface dirt with the soapy lather.
  • For heavier soiling, soak the piece in a small container of warm soapy water for 5–10 minutes.
    • During soaking, the metal findings must remain above the water level.
    • Take the jewellery out, apply soap again with your fingers and gently rub the surface of the jewellery with your fingers.

For heavier soiling, in exceptional cases, the use of a soft-bristled toothbrush is permitted. Apply additional soap to the stained area and use very light rubbing movements. Proceed very delicately.

If the jewellery contains beads, avoid allowing the toothbrush bristles to come into contact with them.

Important!

For heavy soiling, it is strongly recommended to consult the maker before washing.

  • Rinse the jewellery under barely warm running water to wash away all traces of soap.
  • Place the jewellery on a towel on a horizontal surface and use another part of the towel to blot the jewellery from above so that excess water is immediately absorbed into the towel.

The metal findings and the areas where they are attached to the lace must be blotted particularly thoroughly.

If the jewellery accidentally gets wet due to rain or another incident, and there is no heavy soiling, a light washing cycle is sufficient.

Ironing, Lace Stiffening and Drying

  1. Place the jewellery on a flat surface and straighten all the elements with your fingers.

    If the jewellery contains beads, straighten the lace and, if necessary, carefully position the beads in relation to the lace as they were positioned before washing.

    • For jewellery made using the classic tatting technique, place gauze or a similar fabric over the jewellery and iron it through the fabric from the reverse side using steam.
    • For jewellery with beads and multilayer jewellery, also place gauze over the jewellery, but steam it through the gauze.

    Hold the iron or steam generator as close to the fabric as possible and use the steam-shot function evenly over the entire surface of the jewellery. The iron must not be placed directly onto the jewellery.

    • Single-layer jewellery with beads should be steamed from the reverse side.
    • Multilayer jewellery should be steamed first from the reverse side and then from the front side.

    Multilayer brooches do not necessarily need to be steamed — in most cases, it is sufficient to carefully straighten the elements with your fingers and allow the piece to dry in a horizontal position.

    After steaming, be sure to blot excess water from the metal findings.

  2. For shaping and greater stiffness, special lace-stiffening sprays or clear hairspray may be used.

    • After ironing, place the jewellery on a wooden or plastic surface, first making sure that, upon contact with the lace-stiffening product or hairspray, the surface will not transfer colour onto the piece.
    • Finally, straighten the piece and lay it flat on the surface, then evenly apply a small amount of lace-stiffening product (amount according to the product instructions) or hairspray (1–2 sprays) to the reverse side of the jewellery and leave it until completely dry.

Note: Make sure that the product is applied only to the lace. After application, it is recommended to carefully wipe the surfaces of the beads and metal findings so that they remain dry.

The jewellery must be dried only naturally, laid out flat and in a horizontal position.

Drying jewellery on radiators, other sources of heat, or in direct sunlight is prohibited.

Important to Know

Tatting is entirely handmade, so variations in shape, density and tension are part of the uniqueness of each piece.

Natural wear from active use is inevitable. By following these recommendations, the piece will retain its appearance and character for as long as possible.
